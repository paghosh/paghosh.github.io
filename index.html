I am an Associate Professor of Economics at the University of Oklahoma, where I direct the Online MA in Econometrics and previously served as Director of Graduate Studies. I received my Ph.D. in Economics from Syracuse University in 2014, an M.S. in Quantitative Economics from the Indian Statistical Institute, Kolkata, and a B.S. in Economics from the University of Calcutta. Before graduate school I worked for two years as a market research analyst at Hewlett-Packard in Bangalore.

My research spans econometrics, labor economics, health economics, and development economics. On the methods side I have developed unconditional quantile regression under endogeneity and Box-Cox power transformation quantile regressions, and applied them to wage inequality and discrimination. On the applied side I study crime, gender gaps in economics publishing, maternal and child health, vaccine policy, and the funding of the Indian Health Service. My work has appeared in the Journal of Applied Econometrics, Labour Economics, World Development, Journal of Comparative Economics, Southern Economic Journal, Statistical Papers, Value in Health, and Health Affairs Forefront, among others.

I teach across the curriculum, from Principles of Microeconomics to PhD Econometrics and PhD Labor Economics, and I received the University of Oklahoma's Merrick Foundation Teaching Award in 2026. I have chaired or co-chaired four doctoral dissertations and served on eighteen dissertation committees.
