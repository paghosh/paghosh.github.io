I am an Associate Professor of Economics at the University of Oklahoma, where I direct the Online MA in Econometrics and previously served as Director of Graduate Studies. I received my Ph.D. in Economics from Syracuse University in 2014, an M.S. in Quantitative Economics from the Indian Statistical Institute, Kolkata, and a B.S. in Economics from the University of Calcutta. Before graduate school I worked for two years as a market research analyst at Hewlett-Packard in Bangalore.

My research spans econometrics, labor economics, health economics, and development economics. On the methods side I work on unconditional quantile regression, including Box-Cox power transformations and endogenous regressors, and apply these methods to wage inequality and racial wage gaps. On the applied side I study crime, gender gaps in economics publishing, maternal and child health, vaccine policy, and the funding of the Indian Health Service. My work has appeared in the Journal of Applied Econometrics, Labour Economics, World Development, Journal of Comparative Economics, Southern Economic Journal, Statistical Papers, and Value in Health, among others.

I teach across the curriculum, from Principles of Microeconomics to PhD Econometrics and PhD Labor Economics, and I received the University of Oklahoma's Merrick Foundation Teaching Award in 2026. I chair or have chaired four doctoral dissertations and have served on eighteen dissertation committees.
