## Who the program is for

The program is designed for working professionals and recent graduates who want to move into data intensive roles in industry, government, consulting, or research, and for students who plan to apply to doctoral programs in economics or a related field. Students come from economics, business, mathematics, engineering, and the social sciences.

## What students learn

- Applied econometrics from regression through instrumental variables, panel data, difference in differences, regression discontinuity, and quantile methods.
- Machine learning methods for prediction and for causal questions, and how to tell the two apart.
- Python for data analysis, taught from the first course, with R where it is the better tool.
- Hands on projects with real data that mirror the problems analysts face in the labor market, health policy, finance, and public policy.

## Structure

- 30 credit hours, fully online, no campus visits required.
- 15 to 21 months to completion depending on full time or part time enrollment.
- Courses are taught by the same faculty who teach the PhD sequence in the Department of Economics.

## Courses I teach in the program

- **ECON 5213, Advanced Econometrics.** Statistical foundations and regression methods, in Python, with real data (Spring 2025 and 2026).
- **ECON 5083, Applied Labor Economics.** Wage determination, inequality, and policy evaluation, with hands on Python study guides (Summer 2026).
